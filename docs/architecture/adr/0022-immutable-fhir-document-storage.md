# ADR-0022: Immutable FHIR Document Bundles for ABDM exchanges

- **Status:** Proposed
- **Date:** 2026-05-08
- **Deciders:** [Architect], [Engineering Manager], [Co-Tech-Lead]

## Context and problem statement

ABDM Milestone 3 exchanges health information as **FHIR R4 Document Bundles** -- a `Bundle` of type `document` with a leading `Composition` resource that names the document's title, type, sections, and authoring context, followed by referenced clinical resources (`Patient`, `Encounter`, `Observation`, `MedicationRequest`, `DiagnosticReport`, etc.) ([HL7 FHIR R4 -- Documents](https://hl7.org/fhir/R4/documents.html)). NRCeS publishes profiles for OP Consultation Records, Discharge Summaries, Diagnostic Reports, Prescriptions, Health Documents, and others ([NRCeS FHIR Implementation Guide R4](https://nrces.in/ndhm/fhir/r4/index.html)).

The architecture must decide *how* these bundles are produced and persisted when:

- A consultation finalises and the platform, acting as **HIP**, must serve the bundle on a future `health-information.transfer` request from a HIU (potentially months later, governed by a previously granted ABDM consent).
- The platform, acting as **HIU**, receives an external bundle from a remote HIP and must store it for the doctor to view and for medico-legal traceability of *what was disclosed to us*.

Two implementation paradigms exist:

**A. Dynamic generation.** When a HIP `health-information.transfer` request arrives, the platform queries its operational tables (visit, prescription, diagnoses) and constructs a fresh FHIR Document Bundle on the fly.

**B. Immutable artifact.** When a clinical event finalises (e.g., the doctor clicks "Finalize" on a consultation), the platform constructs the FHIR Document Bundle once and stores the byte-exact artifact. On a future `health-information.transfer`, the stored bundle is read and re-emitted unchanged.

Most CRUD-style HIMS implementations default to (A) on instinct ("the data is in the source tables; just re-serialise it"). The production HIMS (`hims-production`'s `abdi-lims-backed`) actually does (B) -- not by sophistication, but because anything else fails ABDM Facilitation Testing in subtle ways. The agent reviews ([g](../../../agent-reviews/g/fhir-care-context-storage-review.md), [t](../../../agent-reviews/t/hld-emr-mrd-abdm-care-context-review/review.md)) explicitly identified (B) as the only correct approach, naming the pattern the *Immutable Document Paradigm*.

This ADR codifies (B) for the new platform.

## Decision drivers

- **FHIR Document semantics.** A FHIR `Bundle` of type `document` is *defined* as a clinical snapshot in time, not a query view. Its `Composition` carries the authoring date, attesting practitioner, and section structure that were true at the time of finalisation ([HL7 FHIR R4 -- Bundle](https://hl7.org/fhir/R4/bundle.html), [HL7 FHIR R4 -- Composition](https://hl7.org/fhir/R4/composition.html)).
- **Temporal drift in source data.**
  - The prescribing doctor's name may change (marriage, professional credential update).
  - The hospital's registered address may change.
  - An ICD-10 code or LOINC code in the master catalog may be deprecated, renamed, or remapped.
  - A drug's master record may be edited, retitled, or split.
  - A doctor's department affiliation may change.
  - Re-serialising "the same consultation" from current state produces a *different* document.
- **Digital signatures.** ABDM dispatches may carry digital signatures of the bundle bytes. A signature over a regenerated document is invalid because the bytes differ. Even where signatures are not currently mandatory, the architecture must not *foreclose* signing.
- **DPDP Act and NABH retention obligations.** The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 ([MeitY publication](https://www.meity.gov.in/writereaddata/files/Digital%20Personal%20Data%20Protection%20Act%202023.pdf)) imposes purpose-limitation and accuracy obligations on a Data Fiduciary; demonstrating *what was disclosed to a HIU* requires a byte-exact retained copy. NABH 5th Edition's Information Management System chapter requires provenance-preserving retention of clinical documents.
- **Audit and dispute resolution.** A patient or HIU disputing the contents of a previously transferred record can only be answered by producing the exact bytes that were transferred. Regenerated bytes cannot answer this.
- **HIU-side retention.** Bundles received from external HIPs are authored elsewhere; the platform has no source data to regenerate them from. They *must* be stored as received.
- **`dataEraseAt` enforcement.** ABDM consent-mandated erasure operates on the stored artifact. If artifacts are never stored (option A), there is nothing to erase and no audit trail of the disclosed bytes.

## Considered options

1. **Dynamic generation (no persistent bundle storage).** Re-serialise on demand from operational tables.
2. **Hybrid: cache regeneration output but treat source tables as authoritative.** Store regenerated bundles as a performance optimisation; re-regenerate when source tables change.
3. **Immutable artifact at finalisation (Immutable Document Paradigm).** Generate once at finalisation, store byte-exactly, never modify, erase per consent.

## Decision outcome

Chosen option: **Immutable artifact at finalisation.**

The platform persists every FHIR Document Bundle it produces (HIP) or receives (HIU) as a byte-exact, append-only artifact in Record Foundation's bundle vault. The vault tables have no `UPDATE` path on the bundle bytes; their lifecycle has only three operations: `INSERT` at finalisation, `READ` on disclosure or display, and `DELETE` (or hard-archive) on consent expiry.

Operational source tables (visit, prescription, diagnosis) remain the source of truth for *current* clinical state. The bundle vault is the source of truth for *what was clinically true at the moment of finalisation*. These are different facts, and both are needed.

### Consequences

**Positive:**

- ABDM Facilitation Testing reproducibility. A test that issues a `health-information.transfer` for a finalised consultation receives the same bytes every time -- a property that dynamic generation cannot guarantee in the presence of master-data churn.
- Digital signatures become possible without further re-architecture. The platform can sign bundles at finalisation time and the signature remains valid for the bundle's retention life.
- Medico-legal posture is strong. "What was disclosed to HIU X on date Y" is answered by reading a single row.
- DPDP Act erasure obligations become tractable. Erasure operates on a discrete, named artifact rather than on a shifting set of underlying source rows.
- HIU-side bundle storage is the same mechanism (just an inbound, not an outbound, artifact). One pattern handles both directions.
- Source-table churn is decoupled from interop integrity. The OPD module is free to evolve its internal schema; the bundles already disclosed remain unchanged.

**Negative / accepted trade-offs:**

- Storage cost. Every finalised consultation, prescription, lab report, and discharge summary is an additional persisted artifact. Mitigation: bundles are JSON, bundles are per-encounter (not per-row), tenants are sharded by Citus, and the volume is bounded by clinical activity (not by user traffic). Volume is well within PostgreSQL's comfortable range; very large bundles can later move to object storage with the row holding only the storage reference.
- "Edit a finalised record" is not directly possible in the vault. ABDM and FHIR address this through the *amendment* pattern: a new bundle is finalised that supersedes the prior one (`Composition.relatesTo` of type `replaces`, [HL7 FHIR R4 -- Composition.relatesTo](https://hl7.org/fhir/R4/composition-definitions.html#Composition.relatesTo)). Both bundles are retained; the timeline displays the latest with the supersession history. The platform must implement this amendment pattern; ad-hoc edit-in-place is structurally rejected.
- Dual write at finalisation. The OPD finalize transaction must produce both the source row update and the bundle artifact. Mitigation: the bundle is produced as part of the `consultation.finalized` event payload by the OPD module, consumed by Record Foundation; the bundle production is in-process at the moment of finalisation, not a downstream batch job.
- Re-projecting timeline read-models is more expensive than re-querying source tables. Mitigation: timeline read-model is a precomputed summary index, rebuildable from event replay if needed.

**Follow-up actions:**

- [ ] Define the `record_foundation.record_bundle_manifests` and `record_foundation.bundle_storage` tables in [Record Foundation LLD](../lld/record-foundation/01-schema-design.md), with explicit no-`UPDATE` discipline (enforced by trigger or by repository code).
- [ ] Define the amendment pattern in the LLD (`Composition.relatesTo` of type `replaces`).
- [ ] Define the bundle storage strategy: PostgreSQL JSONB for v1; object-storage extraction is a future optimisation.
- [ ] Define the digital-signature integration point (deferred to a follow-up ADR; this ADR establishes that signatures are *possible*, not that they are required in v1).
- [ ] Document `dataEraseAt` enforcement: a scheduler reads expired rows and deletes the bundle bytes, leaving an `record_foundation.erasure_log` entry with the consent ID, the original size, the original hash, and the erasure timestamp.

### Why "store the bundle" and not "store enough to regenerate it"

A reasonable engineer asks: "we already store master-data versions; can we just store the master-data version pointers at finalisation time and regenerate the bundle on demand?" This *would* solve temporal drift. It does not solve the other failures:

- Digital signatures still cannot survive regeneration -- the JSON byte-encoding (key order, whitespace, number formatting) varies across re-serialisation runs even when semantic content is identical.
- "What was disclosed" answers are reconstructed, not retrieved. In a dispute this is weaker evidence.
- The extra complexity of "version-pointer-aware regeneration across N master-data sources" is greater than the cost of just storing the bundle.

The principle "store what you need to retrieve, do not derive what must be authoritative" is the correct one here. Both the agent reviews ([g](../../../agent-reviews/g/fhir-care-context-storage-review.md)) and the production HIMS arrived at the same answer.

## Pros and cons of the options

### Dynamic generation (no persistent bundle storage)

- *Good:* No storage overhead.
- *Good:* "Edit a record" naturally re-flows to the next disclosure.
- *Bad:* Temporal drift. Re-serialising a 2-year-old consultation produces a different document than what was clinically true on the day.
- *Bad:* Digital signatures structurally impossible.
- *Bad:* No HIU-side answer (received bundles cannot be regenerated; they were authored elsewhere).
- *Bad:* DPDP / NABH posture is weak. Cannot answer "what was disclosed".
- *Bad:* `dataEraseAt` operates on nothing; there is no artifact to erase.

### Hybrid (cache regeneration; treat source as authoritative)

- *Good:* Performance benefit on repeated transfers.
- *Bad:* All of the above failures still apply when the cache is invalidated by source changes.
- *Bad:* Cache invalidation rules are complex (master-data changes? source-table changes? doctor profile changes?). Easy to get subtly wrong.
- *Bad:* The cached bytes and the regenerated bytes must be identical for signatures to work; achieving deterministic byte equality across separate regeneration runs is non-trivial.

### Immutable artifact at finalisation

- *Good:* Bytes are stable for the artifact's lifetime.
- *Good:* Signatures are possible.
- *Good:* HIU-side and HIP-side use the same persistence pattern.
- *Good:* DPDP / NABH posture is strong.
- *Good:* `dataEraseAt` operates on a discrete, named artifact.
- *Bad:* Storage cost (manageable; bounded by clinical activity).
- *Bad:* Amendment pattern required for clinical corrections (a discipline cost, but a FHIR-standard one).
